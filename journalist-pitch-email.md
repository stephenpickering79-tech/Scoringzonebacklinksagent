## Body (plain text — ready to send)

Hi [First name],

I think there's a story here for you. Long email, but I've written it as a piece you can lift, edit or bin — whichever's easier.

I'm Stephen Pickering. I'm a 3-handicap golfer from Magherafelt in Northern Ireland, living in Dubai, with a full-time job and 15 years in marketing behind me. I have never written a line of code in my life.

Eight weeks ago I sat down at the kitchen table with an idea and a target. The idea was simple: build the golf app I'd been looking for and couldn't find. The target was less so: 1,000 paying users in 12 months. No co-founder. No engineering team. No investors. Evenings and weekends only, around the day job.

Call it the underdog of the golf app industry. That's how it feels from the inside.

This is the story so far.

**The gap**

Around 60% of golf shots happen inside 100 yards. Most golfers spend 80% of their practice on the driving range. Every app I tried was either GPS, a scorecard, or a swing tip — none of them structured your short-game practice or told you whether you were getting better. That's the gap. Most golfers can tell you their handicap to one decimal place, but couldn't tell you their up-and-down percentage from 30 yards. They don't know where their strokes are hiding, because nothing has ever measured it for them.

Scoring Zone is built around that one idea. Structured drills for putting, chipping, pitching, bunker play and distance wedges. Real benchmarks against your handicap bracket. A pressure mode that simulates what actually happens on the course. A short-game handicap that drops as you genuinely improve. Stop guessing. Start measuring. That's the whole pitch.

**The build**

I started on a tool called Lovable, then graduated to Claude Code a few weeks in and haven't looked back. The speed you can move at, the things you can build — it's completely changed how I think about what's possible for someone with a product brain and no engineering training. I direct the AI agents. They do the typing. I keep the parts no machine can do yet — judgement on what golfers actually need, and the relationships with the coaches putting their names behind it.

In eight weeks, working only outside my 9-to-5, I've built and shipped:

- A fully working short-game performance app
- A full marketing site
- A Founders Programme for PGA professionals, with a coaches' dashboard, email automation and payments wired in
- An automated content engine across 50+ keywords
- An AI agent trained on my writing voice and golf knowledge that drafts the daily blog with one human approval
- A complete UI rebuild — what I'm calling a "Whoop UI for golf"

I took the design inspiration from Whoop. Clean, premium, data-led, no clutter. The app has now been redesigned twice in eight weeks — once because the early version wasn't sharp enough, then again to push it further. The first build was functional. The second was better. The third is the one the coaches saw, and the positive feedback has come flooding in since. Golfers want their training tools to feel as serious as the sport. That's what I'm trying to give them.

**The numbers**

- **226 active golfers** in early access
- **51 PGA teaching pros in the United States** signed up to the Founders Programme
- **Website visits up 700% in the last four weeks** — and the curve is heading the right way.
- Last week, **Google Gemini ranked Scoring Zone #1** when asked for the best short-game app of 2026 — the first time a large language model has cited the product by name.

And here's the part that surprised me most: I haven't started marketing publicly yet. There has been no launch, no press, no paid spend, no campaign. The users are arriving on their own — word is getting out on Reddit and the golf forums, golfers are telling other golfers, coaches are telling other coaches. If this is what no marketing looks like, I'm quietly excited about what the next phase looks like.

It's free, early access, no credit card. App Store launch coming. The next phase is full marketing — Reddit, forums, US college golf programmes, an agent that scans the live conversations of the golf internet for relevant threads. The goal hasn't moved: 1,000 paying users in 12 months.

**Why I think it's worth a column**

The interesting thing isn't the app. It's that one person, with a day job and no technical background, can now genuinely compete with venture-backed sports tech. The conversation about AI in the workplace has been overwhelmingly bleak — jobs lost, jobs threatened. This is the other side of it. I'm one man doing the work of fifteen, building something professional coaches are happy to put their reputations behind. If that's possible in golf — a sport notoriously slow to change — it's possible almost anywhere.

I'd love to walk you through any of it. Happy to share full numbers, screen-share a build session, or put you in touch with any of the PGA pros inside the programme. There's no PR person and no script. You'd just get me, the truth of it, and as much access as you want.

If it's not for you, no offence taken. If it is, I'd appreciate a few minutes of your time.
